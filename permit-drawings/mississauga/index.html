Mississauga rules we draw to.

Checked against Zoning By-law 0225-2007 and the City's own pages, as of October 2026. By-laws change often: confirm with the City before you submit.

Up to three units on a lot

A maximum of two attached ARUs, or one attached and one detached ARU, per lot (s.4.1.1.1, By-law 0174-2023).

No extra parking for an ARU

Additional parking isn't required for an attached or detached ARU, and the lot keeps one driveway only (s.4.1.1.2, 4.1.1.4).

Below-grade entrances: rear and interior side yards only

Not permitted in front or exterior side yards, and set back at least 1.2 m from the interior side and rear lot lines (s.4.1.5.8).

Detached ARU size

Up to 55 m² on lots of 550 m² or less; 10% of lot area, to a maximum of 100 m², on larger lots (Table 4.1.1.14).

Decks

A permit is needed over 0.61 m high. In the rear yard a deck may encroach up to 5.0 m but stay 1.5 m from the rear lot line (s.4.1.5.2).

Sheds and accessory buildings

No permit under 15 m² with no plumbing, but zoning still applies. On lots under 750 m²: 3.0 m high, 10 m² per structure, 30 m² combined (Table 4.1.2.2).

Second-unit registration

Second units are registered with the City after the permit closes, under By-law 0114-2016.

Submitting in Mississauga. Mississauga takes applications through ePlans and asks for a metric site plan with site statistics, including the second unit's floor area. We set every sheet up that way.

Questions from Mississauga firms

Do you draw the below-grade entrance with the second unit?

Yes. Most Mississauga basement units need one, and it can only go in a rear or interior side yard, 1.2 m from those lot lines. The entrance is $850 on its own; we price it alongside the second unit on the same job.

Will my client or the City see your name?

No. The set goes out in your title block, under your name. We never contact your client or the municipality.

Can you guarantee zoning approval?

No. We check the set against the zoning by-law for the address and flag anything tight on the job notes sheet, but approval rests with the municipality. By-laws change often, so confirm the current rules with the City before you submit.
