Toronto rules we draw to.

Checked against Zoning By-law 569-2013 and the City's own pages, as of October 2026. By-laws change often: confirm with the City before you submit.

Up to four units city-wide

Multiplexes of two to four units are permitted as-of-right in the R, RD, RS, RM and RT zones; secondary suites are under Chapter 150.10 and garden suites under Chapter 150.7.

Secondary suite size

No more than 45% of the dwelling's interior floor area, except that a suite in the basement of a one-storey detached house may take the whole basement (150.10.40.40).

No parking for the first suite

Duplexes, triplexes and fourplexes haven't needed parking since February 2022; a parking space applies only for each secondary suite beyond the first (By-law 549-2019).

Front entrances

The City says By-law 474-2023 removed the old restriction on secondary-suite entrances in the front wall.

Exterior stairs

Stairs may encroach if no wider than 2.0 m and at least 0.6 m from a lot line (10.5.40.60(3)).

Decks

A permit is needed above 60 cm. A first-storey rear platform may encroach the lesser of 2.5 m or 50% of the required rear setback (10.5.40.60).

Ancillary buildings and garages

Up to 4.0 m high, or 2.5 m within 1.8 m of the house, and not in a front yard (10.5.60).

Trees

Private trees 30 cm or more in diameter at 1.4 m are protected; the set goes in with a Tree Declaration where it applies.

Submitting in Toronto. Toronto takes small residential applications through Toronto Building Online Services. The City targets 10 business days to review a complete house-stream application, so a complete, consistent set matters.

Questions from Toronto firms

Do you draw underpinning for Toronto basements?

Yes. Underpinning or benching drawings are $650, or $400 added to a basement finish or second unit. Your engineer reviews and stamps the set.

Will my client or the City see your name?

No. The set goes out in your title block, under your name. We never contact your client or the municipality.

Can you guarantee zoning approval?

No. We check the set against the zoning by-law for the address and flag anything tight on the job notes sheet, but approval rests with the municipality. By-laws change often, so confirm the current rules with the City before you submit.
